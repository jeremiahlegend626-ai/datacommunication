Transmission Media

Guided and Unguided Channels

← Back to Home

Transmission media are the physical channels through which data signals travel.

Guided Media (Bounded)

  • Twisted Pair Cable: Two copper wires twisted together, commonly used in telephone lines and network cables. Affordable but susceptible to interference.
  • Coaxial Cable: A central conductor surrounded by insulation and a shield, used for cable television and broadband. Better shielding against interference than twisted pair.
  • Fiber Optic Cable: Uses light signals transmitted through glass or plastic fibers. Offers high bandwidth, immunity to electromagnetic interference, and long-distance transmission capabilities.

Unguided Media (Unbounded)

  • Radio Waves: Used for AM/FM radio, mobile phones, and wireless networks. Can penetrate obstacles but are susceptible to interference and fading.
  • Microwaves: Used for satellite communication and point-to-point wireless links. Require line-of-sight propagation.
  • Infrared: Used for short-range communication like remote controls. Cannot penetrate solid objects.
