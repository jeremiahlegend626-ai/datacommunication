Satellite Communication

Communication via Orbiting Satellites

← Back to Home

Satellite communication uses artificial satellites orbiting Earth to relay data signals over long distances and remote areas where terrestrial infrastructure is unavailable.

Key Characteristics

  • Coverage: Satellites can cover vast geographic areas including oceans and mountains, making them ideal for global communication.
  • Types of Orbits:
    • GEO (Geostationary): Satellites remain fixed over a location with high latency (250-300ms).
    • LEO (Low Earth Orbit): Satellites orbit closer to Earth with lower latency but limited coverage area.
    • MEO (Medium Earth Orbit): Middle ground between GEO and LEO.
  • Applications: Television broadcasting, long-distance telephone calls, GPS navigation, weather forecasting, and internet connectivity in remote regions.
  • Advantages: Global coverage, no need for terrestrial infrastructure in remote areas, broadcast capability.
  • Disadvantages: High latency in GEO satellites, weather interference, high initial costs, propagation delay.
