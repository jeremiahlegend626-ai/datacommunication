Digital and Analog Transmission

Comparing Signal Types

Analog Transmission

  • Represents data as continuous wave signals that vary in amplitude, frequency, or phase.
  • Used historically in traditional telephone systems and radio broadcasting.
  • More susceptible to noise and signal degradation over long distances.
  • Requires more bandwidth to transmit information.

Digital Transmission

  • Represents data as discrete binary values (0s and 1s) using voltage levels or signal pulses.
  • Modern standard for data communication, computer networks, and the Internet.
  • More resistant to noise and interference, allowing for error detection and correction.
  • More efficient use of bandwidth and easier to implement encryption for security.
  • Examples: Ethernet networks, fiber optic transmission, 4G/5G mobile networks.

Comparison

Digital transmission has largely replaced analog due to superior noise immunity, better bandwidth utilization, easier amplification without quality loss, and superior security capabilities. However, some legacy systems and applications still use analog transmission.
